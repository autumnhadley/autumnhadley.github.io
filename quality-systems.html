01 / The Objective

Find a trend. Study it

The context, decisions, and reasoning behind the work.

My approach

  1. Understand

    Find the issue affecting the quality of the final result. Create categories to analyze specific issues. Collect a batch of data targeting the issues.

  2. Analyze

    Review and compare the results of the collected data. Create tables and visuals to help understand the trending issues.

  3. Improve

    Share the findings with a detailed report while providing solutions and examples of trending issues to improve the overall quality.

The process in practice

From a finding to a recommendation

Example: Palia · Hair and headwear compatibility

Record the observation
A headband clipped into the crown of some hairstyles and was fully hidden by longer bangs. The Main Log recorded this as a clipping defect with severity 3 — High.
Compare the findings
Hair accounted for 15 recorded issues rated 1–5, the highest count of any asset category. Comparing these findings helped identify hair interactions with headwear and clothing as a testing priority.
Recommend the next step
Extend hairstyle testing across collars, hoods, and headwear. Document when automatic outfit changes restrict customization, so visual defects and design limitations can be addressed separately.

04 / Quality Report

Game Cosmetics Quality Report

More details about the report can be reviewed here ↗ .

Summary

Challenge

This study compares how character cosmetics work together in four 3D games. It looks at visual bugs, customization limits, and opportunities to improve the experience.

Testing Approach

Different combinations of hair, clothing, hats, shoes, and accessories were tested. Results were recorded in the QA Log, with each bug rated by severity.

The table summarizes each game's findings, most common problem area, and average bug severity.

Findings Breakdown

Testing produced 50 findings across four games. Of these, 32 were bugs. The other 18 covered customization limits, menu issues, and features that worked well.

Confirmed Defects — 32 findings

Visual bugs or items that did not work correctly. Each bug was rated from 1 (minor) to 5 (major).

Design Limitations — 10 findings

Limits on what players can combine, such as a shirt disappearing when a jacket is equipped. These are not always bugs.

UI/UX Limitations — 4 findings

Menu features that could be easier to find or use.

Positive Observations — 4 findings

Examples where items worked well together or adjusted to prevent clipping.

Paralives had the most findings, but also offered more customization options to test. A higher finding count does not automatically mean lower game quality.

05 / Reflection

What I’d carry forward.

Conclusion

These results reflect the combinations tested, rather than every possible outfit or the overall quality of each game.

Across the four games, hair assets caused the most cosmetic compatibility issues, especially when paired with hats, glasses, and clothing. Some games avoided clipping by changing or hiding parts of an outfit or hairstyle. While this helped items fit together, it sometimes limited the player's customization choices.

The findings point to two useful areas for improvement:

  1. Testing more combinations of overlapping items
  2. Making sure automatic changes do not unnecessarily restrict player choice
